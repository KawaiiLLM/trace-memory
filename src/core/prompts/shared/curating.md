The commonest ways the principles above are broken in the existing knowledge, ranked by frequency and harm. The list adds no principle; judge by the principles, and handle anything not listed by them.

1. **Running log**: who did what in which ticket or commit, test counts, deployment steps, run costs, timelines. Rewrite as the rulings, mechanisms, reasons, lessons and one entry point that still hold; with nothing left to keep, archive as inadmissible. (Keep no running log, Useful afterwards)
2. **`open` used as a ledger**: the work is finished or superseded, or the question was settled elsewhere, yet the item stays `open`. (Choose the category by use, State what is unresolved)
   - Settled: rewrite it as its conclusion under the same identity and recategorize it.
   - Fully covered by another current item: merge it into that item.
   - Still open: keep the question or risk, current grounds, entry point and the essentials of any clear resolution condition.
   - Pure progress: archive it as inadmissible.
3. **Duplicates**: several items say the same thing, including verbatim copies across categories and unresolved "concurrent conflict, created instead" notes. Merge when the scope is the same and the result can be maintained as one, naming the fuller item as primary. Correct a category first only where it is really wrong; never change a scope to make a merge possible. (Maintenance: Merge)
4. **Superseded or contradictory statements still current**: two current statements about one object disagree; something implemented still reads "not yet implemented"; a dated temporary rule has expired. Check conditions, versions and grounds: update or archive what is confirmed superseded, naming the successor; keep both sides, their grounds and background of an undecidable contradiction, merged as `open`. (Update, Merge, Archive)
5. **An agent's derivation given as a user ruling**: the item presents what an agent decided, derived or proposed, or what the user never answered, as the user's ruling. Correct the attribution and keep the strength of evidence; split only where the parts need independent maintenance and each meets admission, never just because the sources differ. (Distinguish evidence, Judge claim by claim)
6. **Several topics in one item**: topics that need separate maintenance share one item. Split by independent maintenance, not mechanically by category; an item that is only too long is first compressed under Insufficient length. (Division, Insufficient length)
7. **Appended history**: "also:", "later:", "then tightened", dates in sequence. Rewrite from the current view as conclusion and reasons; keep only the times and conditions that affect meaning or judgment. (Do not stack history, Preserve qualifiers)
8. **Identifiers or shorthand only**: a ticket or task number as the subject, or telegraphic shorthand that cannot be read outside the original discussion. Rewrite as a rule and reason that stand without the number; archive what is only "X was implemented" as inadmissible. (Understandable outside the original discussion)
9. **Unreliable pointers**: the body depends on a temporary directory or an archived item that cannot be reliably read back. Replace it with a reliable entry point, such as a repository document or a fact id, and keep the essentials needed to understand in the body. (Only loses detail → leave it in place, Insufficient length)
10. **Wrong scope or category**: single-project or one-off content in the global scope; a legacy category. Update the scope or category; archive a one-off snapshot as inadmissible. (Choose the category by use)
