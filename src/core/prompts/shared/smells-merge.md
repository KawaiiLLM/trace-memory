**Bad smells when merging knowledge**

The commonest ways the existing principles are broken in real records, ranked by frequency and harm, to be checked item by item. The list adds no principle; judge by the principles, and handle anything not listed by them.

1. **Duplicates**: several items say the same thing, including verbatim copies across categories and unresolved "concurrent conflict, created instead" notes. Merge when the scope is the same and the result can be maintained as one, naming the fuller item as primary. A category that is really wrong is left for the next update round to correct, and the merge round handles it after that; never change a scope to make a merge possible. (Merge)
2. **An `open` item already covered**: the open question is fully covered by another current item. Merge it into that item. (Merge, State what is unresolved)
3. **A contradiction that cannot be settled**: two current statements disagree; check conditions, versions and grounds first. Only when it is one object under the same conditions and the contradiction cannot be settled, merge them into one `open` item that keeps both sides, their grounds and background. The scope must be the same and the result maintainable as one. (Preserve disputes, Merge)
4. **The merged body is distorted**: a ruling, condition or qualifier that still holds in an absorbed item did not reach the primary, or a claim, attribution or ground does not match. Restore or correct it; where length is short, remove redundancy first and leave a pointer. (Preserve qualifiers, Distinguish evidence, Insufficient length)
