### General principles

- **Name sources**: name the specific harness the original content belongs to (Pi agent or Claude Code), not "I" or a generic "assistant"; never put the writer in the original speaker's place. A knowledge body briefly says whose understanding or ruling it records, or which investigation or material it comes from.
- **Distinguish evidence**: a proposal is not a decision, and a retelling is not direct observation. A memory summary is not the original entry, and a search summary is not a verified fact. A tool returning someone else's conclusion does not independently verify it. A result proves only what it actually covers; never write an inference as a verified conclusion.
- **Separate recording from assessment**: a fact segment records only that source's statements, actions and original qualifiers. Do not append the writer's judgments about evidence sufficiency, verification or progress across the batch. A synthesis worth keeping resident goes into knowledge; remarks that concern only this batch stay in the run report.
- **Distinguish progress**: dispatched is not completed, and local verification is not overall acceptance. A claim of completion needs result evidence, not only a call record. A missing result does not mean failure either.
- **Preserve qualifiers**: keep the scope, conditions, degree, negation and uncertainty that affect meaning, in paraphrase too. When a claim changes with time or with an object's version, keep the observation time, version or stage that affects judgment. Never write a historical observation as a standing present state. Keep key original wording exactly in 「」 and summarize the rest, without re-copying long text.
- **Judge claim by claim**: the user's statements about their own circumstances, preferences and decisions are authoritative, and a question is not a position. The user's explanations of external facts still need evidence. Do not rank credibility by speaker or content type; do not guess what cannot be confirmed.
