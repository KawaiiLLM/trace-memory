### Completeness

- Make the topic understandable without the original discussion. Keep necessary background, current understanding, conditions, reasons, mechanisms, sources and evidence strength. A reader should recognize it, make an initial judgment and know when and where to investigate.
- A name and address alone are insufficient. Once understanding and retrieval are possible, do not keep adding detail.
- Preserve useful lessons. The latest understanding is not merely the last conclusion. Why a rejected approach failed and how that became known may still matter; an unimplemented idea may leave useful understanding.
- Process that explains a mechanism or cause is not useless process to discard.
- Rewrite the complete body from the current perspective, rather than appending new states to old narratives. Superseded drafts and process that no longer affect judgment belong in earlier versions, facts and Raw.

**Example: a decision with its reason.** Illustrative only, not evidence or an admission rule.

> Material: The user approves separating character dialogue from gameplay progression rules so different modes share one interaction model. The final specification is Character Interaction Design.
> Too thin: Character Interaction Design is approved; see the document.
> Useful knowledge: The user approved separating character dialogue from gameplay progression rules so different modes can share the interaction model. See Character Interaction Design for the current specification and details.

Keep the current understanding, reason and retrieval target, rather than appending the superseded drafts or leaving only a document name.
