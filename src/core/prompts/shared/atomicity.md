### Atomicity

- One record, one object, one claim that can be approved, negated, verified or changed on its own, however small. Its conditions and its necessary reason travel with it.
- Atomicity sets how many records; granularity sets how much detail each carries. Granularity never merges claims.
- Sources are never blended. A fact or a body may present the user's, the assistant's and an observation's claims side by side — the two sides of a conflict, a proposal beside the design it would change. It never fuses them into one claim held jointly, unless it records their consensus.
- Repeated similar operations are one record.
- Split only what needs separate maintenance, and only into parts each understood alone. Length alone is no reason to split.
- Present tense, stated so that it guides action.
