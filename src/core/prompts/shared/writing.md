**Writing**

- **Choose the category by use**: decide admission and the boundaries of independent maintenance first, then classify. `open` takes only long-standing unresolved questions and known risks; classify everything else by its main use. Never take the category from a status word or from how fast the content changes. When use changes, classify again without creating a new identity.
- **Understandable outside the original discussion**: keep the necessary background, current understanding, conditions, reasons, mechanisms, sources and evidence strength. A reader should recognize the topic, make an initial judgment, and know when to search and where to look. A name and an address alone are not enough; once understanding and locating are possible, stop adding detail.
- **Keep useful lessons**: the latest understanding is not the last concluding sentence. After an old route is rejected, why it failed and how that became known may still affect judgment; an unimplemented idea may also leave understanding. Process that explains mechanism and cause is not useless process to delete.
- **Do not stack history**: rewrite the complete body from the current perspective, instead of appending the present state after the old narrative. Superseded drafts and process that do not affect current judgment stay in old versions, facts and Raw entries.
- **State what is unresolved**: an `open` item states the question or risk, the current grounds and where to look. When a gap is clear, keep the evidence, ruling or release condition needed to resolve it; do not invent a gap, and do not record investigation progress.
- **Preserve disputes**: when claims about the same object under the same conditions contradict each other and cannot be settled, keep both accounts, their grounds and the dispute's background. Do not pick a side or combine them into a certain conclusion. Missing evidence alone is not a dispute; do not invent one.
